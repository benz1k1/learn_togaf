## The cycle

The ADM is drawn as a circle of phases with Requirements Management in the centre:

1. **Preliminary Phase**: set up the Architecture Capability (before the cycle proper).
2. **A. Architecture Vision**: scope, stakeholders, vision, Statement of Architecture Work.
3. **B. Business Architecture**, **C. Information Systems Architectures** (Data and
   Application), **D. Technology Architecture**: the three development phases, each with the
   same nine steps.
4. **E. Opportunities and Solutions**: the first Architecture Roadmap, Transition Architectures.
5. **F. Migration Planning**: the finalised Implementation and Migration Plan.
6. **G. Implementation Governance**: the Architecture Contract and compliance.
7. **H. Architecture Change Management**: keeping the architecture alive; deciding when to go
   round again.
8. **Requirements Management**: continuous, at the centre, feeding every phase.

Use the ADM explorer to click through the phases; the *inputs versus outputs* drill on each
phase page is where Part 1 loses its marks.

## Adapting the ADM

The method is generic and the standard expects it to be tailored (ADM §1): to fit other
frameworks already in use, to the maturity of the organisation, to the scope of the work
(enterprise, segment, capability), to the level of detail, to the time horizon, and to the
architecture assets already in the repository. Tailoring happens in the Preliminary Phase and
is recorded in the Tailored Architecture Framework.

## Iteration, scope and the repository

The cycle is iterative at three levels: around the whole cycle, between phases, and within a
phase. The four named iteration cycles are covered in unit F6. Every phase reads the
**Architecture Repository** for reusable assets (reference models, patterns, standards, the
Architecture Landscape) and writes its results back, which is why the Preliminary Phase creates
the repository before Phase A starts.
