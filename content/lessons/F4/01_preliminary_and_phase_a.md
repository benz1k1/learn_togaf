## Preliminary Phase: where, what, why, who, how

The Preliminary Phase answers how *this* enterprise will do architecture. Its objectives are to
**determine** and to **establish** the Architecture Capability. Its steps (ADM §2):

1. Scope the enterprise organisations impacted.
2. Confirm governance and support frameworks.
3. Define and establish the EA team and organisation.
4. Identify and establish architecture principles.
5. Tailor the TOGAF framework (and other frameworks).
6. Develop a strategy and plan for tools and techniques.

Outputs to remember: the **Organizational Model for EA**, the **Tailored Architecture
Framework** (with the principles), the **initial Architecture Repository**, and possibly the
**Request for Architecture Work**. Trap: principles are *established* here; Phase A *confirms
and elaborates* them.

## Phase A: from a request to an approved statement of work

Phase A starts when a **Request for Architecture Work** arrives and ends when the sponsor
approves the **Statement of Architecture Work**. Its objectives are a high-level aspirational
**Architecture Vision** of the value to be delivered, and approval of the work that will
deliver it.

The steps, in order (ADM §3): establish the project; identify stakeholders, concerns and
business requirements; confirm goals, drivers and constraints; evaluate capabilities; assess
readiness for business transformation; define scope; confirm principles; develop the
Architecture Vision; define value propositions and KPIs; identify transformation risks; develop
the Statement of Architecture Work and secure approval.

Outputs: the approved Statement of Architecture Work, the Architecture Vision, refined
principles, a **Capability Assessment**, a **Communications Plan**, and a start on the
Architecture Definition Document. The techniques that live here: **Stakeholder Management**
(the stakeholder map and the power/interest grid), **Business Scenarios**, **Business
Transformation Readiness Assessment** and **Risk Management**.
