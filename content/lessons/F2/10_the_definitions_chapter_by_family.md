Chapter 4 of the Introduction holds 89 defined terms; a supplementary glossary in Appendix B
covers the rest, and Merriam-Webster is the fallback for anything undefined (INTRO §4). The
Foundation exam asks for these definitions almost word for word, so the fastest way to hold them is
by family. Every definition below is paraphrased; the flashcards carry the same terms.

## Architecture, frameworks and methods

| Term | Meaning, in short | Ref |
|---|---|---|
| Architecture | two senses: (1) from ISO 42010, an entity's fundamental concepts or properties, seen in its environment, together with the principles that govern how it is realised and evolves; (2) the structure of components, their relationships and the principles guiding their design and evolution | INTRO §4.8 |
| Architecture Framework | a conceptual structure for planning, developing, implementing, governing and sustaining an architecture | INTRO §4.13 |
| Framework | a structure for content or process used to structure thinking and ensure consistency and completeness | INTRO §4.47 |
| Architecture Development Method | the core of the framework: a multi-phase, iterative approach to developing and using an EA to shape and govern business transformation | INTRO §4.11 |
| Method | a defined, repeatable approach to a type of problem | INTRO §4.56 |
| Metamodel | a model describing the entities used to build an Architecture Description, their characteristics and key relationships | INTRO §4.55 |
| Architecture Model | a representation of a subject of interest, smaller-scale, simplified or abstract | INTRO §4.17 |
| Pattern | a technique for putting building blocks into context, e.g. a reusable solution to a problem; blocks are what you use, patterns say how, when, why and with what trade-offs | INTRO §4.60 |
| Reference Model | an abstract framework for understanding significant relationships among entities in an environment, supporting consistent standards; not tied to concrete implementations (from OASIS) | INTRO §4.64 |
| Enterprise | the highest level of description of an organisation, typically covering all missions and functions; often spans several organisations | INTRO §4.43 |

## Domains

| Term | Meaning, in short | Ref |
|---|---|---|
| Architecture Domain | the architectural area being considered; four primary domains (business, data, application, technology), others (motivation, security, governance) span them | INTRO §4.12 |
| Business Architecture | whole-of-business, multi-dimensional views of capabilities, end-to-end value delivery, information and organisational structure, and how those relate to strategies, products, policies, processes, initiatives and stakeholders | INTRO §4.27 |
| Data Architecture | the structure of the enterprise's major data types and sources, logical and physical data assets, and data management resources | INTRO §4.39 |
| Application Architecture | how the applications that deliver key business capabilities and look after the data assets are structured and interact | INTRO §4.3 |
| Technology Architecture | the structure and interaction of technology services and technology components | INTRO §4.81 |
| Solution Architecture | a description of a discrete, focused business operation and how IS/IT supports it | INTRO §4.73 |
| Digital Architecture | an inclusive architecture combining EA with data science, telecoms and IoT, security, AI, cognitive science, robotics and social media to deliver operational services | INTRO §4.41 |

## Building blocks and continua

| Term | Meaning, in short | Ref |
|---|---|---|
| Building Block | a potentially reusable component that combines with others to deliver architectures and solutions | INTRO §4.26 |
| Architecture Building Block | an architectural component that specifies the required SBBs at a more logical, supplier-independent level | INTRO §4.9 |
| Solution Building Block | a physical or implementation-specific component that realises part or all of one or more ABBs; business, application and technology SBBs exist | INTRO §4.74 |
| Enterprise Continuum | a categorisation mechanism for classifying architecture and solution building blocks as they evolve from generic to specific (or the reverse) | INTRO §4.45 |
| Architecture Continuum | a categorisation mechanism, with increasing detail and specialisation, for components and artifacts held in the Architecture Landscape or Reference Library; from reference models and basic blocks through industry to organisation-specific | INTRO §4.10 |
| Solutions Continuum | the same mechanism for what is held in the Solutions Landscape or Reference Library | INTRO §4.75 |
| Foundation Architecture | generic building blocks, their relationships, and the principles and guidelines that more specific architectures build on | INTRO §4.46 |

## Work products

| Term | Meaning, in short | Ref |
|---|---|---|
| Artifact | an architectural work product that describes an aspect of the architecture | INTRO §4.23 |
| Deliverable | an architectural work product that is contractually specified and then formally reviewed, agreed and signed off by stakeholders | INTRO §4.40 |
| Architecture Vision | a succinct description of the Target Architecture, its business value and the changes it brings; an aspirational vision and a boundary for detailed development | INTRO §4.22 |
| Roadmap | an abstracted plan for business or technology change, usually across disciplines and years | INTRO §4.66 |
| Work Package | a defined set of actions that achieves one or more business objectives; sized as part of a project, a whole project, or a programme | INTRO §4.89 |
| Requirement | a statement of need that is unambiguous, testable or measurable, and necessary for acceptability | INTRO §4.65 |
| Gap | a statement of difference between two states, used in gap analysis between Baseline and Target | INTRO §4.48 |
| Objective | an organisational aim stated in a SMART way | INTRO §4.59 |

## Views and stakeholders

| Term | Meaning, in short | Ref |
|---|---|---|
| Stakeholder | an individual, team, organisation, or class of them, with an interest in a system | INTRO §4.76 |
| Concern | an interest in a system relevant to one or more stakeholders; may cover performance, reliability, security, evolvability and may decide acceptability | INTRO §4.37 |
| Architecture View | a depiction of a system seen from the standpoint of a related set of concerns | INTRO §4.20 |
| Architecture Viewpoint | the specified conventions for one kind of view, effectively that view's definition or schema | INTRO §4.21 |
| Model Kind | conventions for a type of modelling; a viewpoint references model kinds, a view incorporates models | INTRO §4.58 |
| Taxonomy of Architecture Views | the organised collection of all views pertinent to an architecture | INTRO §4.80 |
| Viewpoint Library | the collection of viewpoint specifications in the Reference Library part of the repository | INTRO §4.88 |
| Actor | a person, organisation or system with one or more roles that initiates or interacts with activities; internal or external | INTRO §4.2 |
| Role | the expected behaviour of an actor, or the part someone plays in a process; an actor may have several | INTRO §4.67 |

## States and levels

| Term | Meaning, in short | Ref |
|---|---|---|
| Baseline | a specification formally reviewed and agreed, the basis for further change, alterable only through formal change control | INTRO §4.24 |
| Target Architecture | the description of a future state of the architecture; several may exist along a roadmap | INTRO §4.79 |
| Transition Architecture | a formal description of the architecture as it will stand at one architecturally significant moment; a sequence of them shows the path from Baseline to Target | INTRO §4.84 |
| Architecture Landscape | the architectural picture of the assets an enterprise uses or plans to use, taken at particular points in time | INTRO §4.15 |
| Architecture Level | a framework for dividing the Landscape into levels of granularity; distinct from partitions | INTRO §4.16 |
| Architecture Partition | a subset of architecture produced by dividing it to ease development and management | INTRO §4.18 |
| Strategic Architecture | a formal summary of the whole enterprise that organises operational and change activity and gives executives a long-term view for setting direction | INTRO §4.78 |
| Segment Architecture | a detailed, formal description of areas within an enterprise, used at programme or portfolio level to organise and align change | INTRO §4.68 |
| Capability Architecture | an architecture describing the abilities an enterprise possesses | INTRO §4.34 |
| Capability Increment | a discrete portion of a capability architecture that delivers specific value; once all increments are done, the capability is realised | INTRO §4.35 |

## Capabilities, services and governance

| Term | Meaning, in short | Ref |
|---|---|---|
| Capability | an ability that an organisation, person or system possesses (general-purpose) | INTRO §4.33 |
| Business Capability | a particular ability a business may possess or exchange to achieve a specific purpose | INTRO §4.28 |
| Value Stream | a depiction of the end-to-end set of activities that together produce a result for a customer, stakeholder or end user | INTRO §4.85 |
| Business Model | a model of the rationale for how an enterprise creates, delivers and captures value | INTRO §4.31 |
| Course of Action | direction and focus from strategic goals and objectives, often to deliver the business model's value proposition | INTRO §4.38 |
| Service | an encapsulated element of behaviour providing specific functionality in response to requests from actors or other services; has an interface and description | INTRO §4.69 |
| Business Service | a unique element of business behaviour, packaged to support the business | INTRO §4.32 |
| Application Service | a discrete behaviour requestable from an application, supporting part or all of one or more business services | INTRO §4.6 |
| Technology Service | a technical capability required to provide enabling infrastructure supporting application delivery | INTRO §4.83 |
| Governance | the discipline of overseeing and steering how a business (or an IS/IT landscape) is managed so that it delivers the outcomes required | INTRO §4.49 |
| Architecture Governance | the practice of monitoring and directing architecture-related work to deliver outcomes and adhere to principles, standards and roadmaps | INTRO §4.14 |
| Architecture Principle | a qualitative statement of intent that the architecture should meet | INTRO §4.19 |
| Interoperability | the ability to share information and services; or of systems to exchange and use information; or to provide and receive services from each other | INTRO §4.52 |
| Logical / Physical | implementation-independent / real-world and tangible; a logical architecture is realised through a physical one | INTRO §4.53, INTRO §4.61 |
